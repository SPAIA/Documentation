# Experiment: Fixed-Camera Habitat Feature and Change Detection

**Project:** SPAIA  
**Status:** Proposed quantitative validation; qualitative feasibility explored  
**Date:** 22 November 2026  
**Suggested repository path:** `experiments/fixed-camera-habitat-change-detection.md`  
**Version:** 0.1 (experiment design)

## Summary

Evaluate whether repeated RGB photographs, particularly those taken by SPAIA devices from fixed viewpoints, can produce **reproducible, structured measurements of visible habitat features and changes over time**. A vision-language model (VLM) will identify features and their observable states from a controlled vocabulary. An image segmentation model, initially Meta's Segment Anything Model 3 (SAM 3) as a candidate, will be evaluated for estimating visible feature coverage in consistent regions of interest (ROIs). Time, weather and camera metadata will contextualise changes. **Ecological scores will be calculated in code from versioned rules, not generated by the model.**

The goal is to describe *what changed* and how certain that observation is, while avoiding unsupported explanations of *why* it changed. In a later stage, these records can be compared with insect activity measured by SPAIA devices and with documented land-management interventions.

## Progress to date

- SPAIA has run an initial, simple habitat-photo assessment using **DeepSeek V4** (via the `deepseek-flash` model currently used in the application).
- Results have been checked in field tests and were considered useful/plausible **qualitatively**. This is an initial feasibility indication, **not quantitative validation**.
- No systematic expert-labelled benchmark, segmentation accuracy measurement, repeated-measures reliability analysis, or calibrated comparison with insect activity has yet been established.
- This experiment extends the previous single-image assessment to fixed-vocabulary feature extraction and repeat-photo change detection.

## Research question and hypotheses

**Primary question:** Can a model-plus-segmentation pipeline detect changes in predefined visible habitat features across repeat photographs accurately and consistently enough for longitudinal monitoring?

**Hypotheses to test**

1. Fixed-viewpoint pairs produce more reliable feature-level change detection than loosely aligned handheld pairs.
2. A compact local VLM (Qwen3.5-9B candidate) can approach the accuracy of the existing DeepSeek Flash baseline on a tightly constrained feature taxonomy, even if its general visual reasoning is weaker.
3. Segmentation-derived coverage estimates are more repeatable and numerically accurate than free-form percentages requested from a VLM.
4. Consistent capture time, camera settings, image registration and quality checks reduce apparent changes caused by lighting or viewpoint shifts.
5. Time/weather context improves **interpretation and filtering** of changes, but can also bias models to claim changes they cannot see. It must therefore be tested separately from image-only extraction.

## Scope and non-goals

**In scope (first experiment)**

- Ground-level/near-ground RGB images of gardens and managed green spaces.
- Fixed camera viewpoints, with some intentionally shifted-viewpoint control pairs.
- A versioned, limited feature vocabulary; feature presence, visible condition and changes.
- Percent of **visible pixels in a defined ROI** assigned to selected feature masks, including measurement uncertainty.
- Structured, machine-readable observations and coded change events.
- Comparison against human-review labels and repeatability controls.

**Out of scope for the first experiment**

- Species-level plant identification, pesticide-use inference, or claims about nesting occupancy.
- A validated biodiversity or habitat-quality score.
- Claims that image coverage is ground-area coverage or that canopy cover change necessarily reflects growth.
- Causal attribution of changes to drought, mowing or management interventions.
- Establishing correlations or causal effects on insect populations. This is a later validation track.

## Proposed observation vocabulary (v0.1)

The taxonomy should be reviewed with an ecologist before freezing. Individual feature layers can overlap, so **coverage percentages are not expected to sum to 100%**.

| Group | Feature ID | Observable definition | Candidate output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ground cover | `grass` | Visible grass-like cover | presence, coverage |
| Ground cover | `herbaceous_vegetation` | Non-woody vegetation other than grass where distinguishable | presence, coverage |
| Ground cover | `shrub_foliage` | Visible foliage associated with shrubs/low woody plants | presence, coverage |
| Ground cover | `bare_soil` | Visible exposed soil or substrate, excluding pavement | presence, coverage |
| Ground cover | `leaf_litter` | Fallen leaves on ground | presence, coverage |
| Ground cover | `deadwood` | Visible logs, branches or woody debris | presence, coverage |
| Phenology | `open_flowers` | Visible open blossoms, not species identification | presence, coverage/count class |
| Condition | `brown_dry_vegetation` | Visibly yellow/brown or apparently dry vegetation | presence, coverage |

**Future candidates:** paved surface, standing water, dense structural vegetation, buds, senescent flowers, vegetation height class, seed heads, cavities. Add only when definitions and labels are reliable.

**Allowed change types (v0.1):** `appeared`, `disappeared`, `increased`, `decreased`, `unchanged`, `uncertain`. Terms such as `bloomed`, `wilted` or `greened` can be derived or introduced later only with operational definitions and validation. A model may return `unknown` or `not_assessable` for individual features.

## Proposed pipeline

1. **Capture and metadata.** Collect original photographs with device ID, site ID, timestamp/time zone, camera/lens/settings and available microclimate data. Supplement with historic weather (especially recent rainfall and temperature history), retaining provenance and missingness.
2. **Quality control and registration.** Check blur, glare, rain on lens, occlusion, exposure/white-balance shifts and camera movement. Register images to a fixed reference when practical; use a stable common ROI. Mark pairs unsuitable for quantitative comparison when alignment is inadequate.
3. **Image-only feature extraction.** Supply the pair (or each individual image) to a VLM with a strict predefined vocabulary and schema. Require observable evidence, uncertainty and an explicit `no_change` possibility. Do **not** provide weather to this pass.
4. **Segmentation and coverage.** Evaluate SAM 3 prompted for the exact feature concepts. Calculate mask coverage in pixels within the comparable ROI. SAM masks are proposed measurements, not ground truth; record the model/prompt version and validate ambiguous classes. Use separate masks for overlapping concepts.
5. **Deterministic changes.** Store feature states separately for each photo; compute deltas (percentage points where applicable), categorical changes and thresholds in code. Keep direct VLM pairwise change judgments as a separate comparison output, not the source of numeric delta.
6. **Contextual interpretation (separate pass).** Join image-derived observations with day of year, solar/time-of-day context, sensor data and weather history. Generate only controlled interpretation tags such as `consistent_with_seasonal_change`, `possible_moisture_stress`, or `insufficient_evidence`, if tested and approved. Context must not overwrite raw image observations.
7. **Scoring and storage.** Apply a versioned, transparent scoring formula in application code, once the underlying features are validated. Preserve raw images, masks, feature records, model version, prompt version, timestamps, settings and provenance to allow reprocessing.

## Data contract (illustrative)

```ts
// Nullable measurement means unknown/unassessable, not zero.
type FeatureId =
  | 'grass'
  | 'herbaceous_vegetation'
  | 'shrub_foliage'
  | 'bare_soil'
  | 'leaf_litter'
  | 'deadwood'
  | 'open_flowers'
  | 'brown_dry_vegetation';

type ChangeType =
  | 'appeared'
  | 'disappeared'
  | 'increased'
  | 'decreased'
  | 'unchanged'
  | 'uncertain';

type Observation = {
  imageId: string;
  siteId: string;
  capturedAt: string; // ISO 8601 with offset
  taxonomyVersion: string;
  roiId: string;
  qualityFlags: string[];
  features: Array<{
    id: FeatureId;
    presence: 'present' | 'absent' | 'unknown';
    coveragePct: number | null; // computed from segmentation only
    coverageMethod: 'segmentation' | null;
    confidence: number | null; // not assumed calibrated
  }>;
};

type FeatureChange = {
  fromImageId: string;
  toImageId: string;
  feature: FeatureId;
  change: ChangeType;
  deltaPercentagePoints: number | null; // computed from two observations
  comparable: boolean;
  uncertaintyReason?: string;
};
```

Metadata should be stored in companion records, including `airTemperatureC`, `relativeHumidityPct`, recent rainfall totals, solar/capture timing, data source and whether values are observed, interpolated or missing. A record must also identify the VLM, segmentation weights, prompts, inference settings and processing code version. **Do not treat model-reported confidence as a calibrated probability.**

## Experiment design

### Data collection

**Initial target (adjust as collection permits):**

- Three fixed camera sites representing meaningfully different vegetation/management conditions.
- One quality-controlled reference capture per site per day, ideally over 4–6 weeks; retain extra frames when available to quantify within-day variability.
- Approximately 50–100 **human-reviewed image pairs** spanning small/large real changes, flowering, browning, vegetation growth/occlusion, mowing or disturbance where available, and explicitly unchanged controls.
- If feasible, obtain a few repeat photographs spanning seasons or known management interventions; label the event independently from the photos.

**Controls:** identical image A=A; same-day pairs with light/shadow changes; pairs with small camera shifts; duplicate runs of the same pair; and comparisons after quality filtering. Include both positives and negatives for each feature; ensure rare features are not evaluated only on easy examples.

### Human reference labels

For each held-out image pair, reviewers annotate a common ROI, feature presence per image, visible condition and change direction. For a representative subset, annotate reference masks or polygons to validate coverage. Record disagreements; seek a second reviewer for ambiguous or difficult cases. Where experts disagree or image evidence is insufficient, permit an `uncertain` reference label instead of forcing consensus.

Avoid using DeepSeek outputs as ground truth. Keep training/development and final test sets separate by **site and/or time window**, not just random image pairs, to reduce leakage from near-duplicate captures.

### Model arms

| Arm | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Existing `deepseek-flash` workflow (DeepSeek V4 test baseline) | Continuity with qualitative field tests |
| Qwen3.5-9B multimodal, quantized local inference | Test low-cost, privacy-compatible deployment |
| Optional independent hosted vision model, exact model/version recorded | Check whether conclusions depend on one model family |
| SAM 3 feature segmentation | Test numerical visible-coverage measurement |
| Image-only vs image + environmental-context interpretation | Quantify benefit/bias from contextual metadata |

Hold image resolution, crop/ROI, schema, prompt intent and temperature/repetition settings as comparable as each API permits. Record what differs, including image preprocessing and any model-specific constraints.

### Evaluation metrics

| Outcome | Suggested measure |
| --- | --- |
| Feature presence | Per-feature precision, recall and F1 against reviewer labels |
| Direction of change | Per-feature confusion matrix, macro-F1, and false-change rate on unchanged controls |
| Numeric coverage | Mask IoU/Dice on annotated subset; absolute coverage error in percentage points |
| Repeatability | Variation across repeated runs and adjacent captures with no expected ecological change |
| Viewpoint/lighting robustness | Performance breakdown by alignment and capture-quality strata |
| Unsupported inferences | Rate of claims about unobservable causes or features |
| Practical deployment | Latency per pair, throughput, memory requirement and cost per assessment |

**Exploratory go/no-go criteria (to pre-register before the held-out evaluation):** successful strict-schema outputs; rare but nonzero false-change rates on A=A controls; acceptable agreement on the common features and coverage errors small enough for the intended change thresholds. Select numerical thresholds with ecological reviewers after pilot annotation, rather than retrofitting them to results.

## Weather and temporal design

Keep two outputs distinct:

- **Visual observations:** what is actually visible in image A or B, obtained without environmental cues.
- **Contextual assessment:** whether seasonality, temperatures, rainfall, humidity and management records offer *plausible interpretations* or explain why a comparison is unreliable.

Compare a blinded image-only arm against a context-aware interpretation arm. Never assume that hot/dry conditions prove drought damage or that flowering necessarily means management improvement. If available, use matched dates and historical weather for year-on-year comparisons rather than raw seasonal differences.

## Relationship to insect monitoring and citizen science

In a **later, separately evaluated study**, link habitat-state time series to SPAIA insect activity, matched by site, time of day, weather, season and recording effort. The objective is to ask whether habitat changes are associated with measured activity, not to assume that visual habitat scores are biodiversity outcomes.

Citizen-submitted photographs can eventually reuse the same vocabulary, with weaker guarantees about geometry and coverage. Reports of management actions logged by participants can supply independent event metadata. Fixed-camera measurements, citizen reports and sensor-derived insect activity should remain separate evidence streams so they can corroborate or challenge one another.

## Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
| --- | --- |
| Changes in illumination mistaken for vegetation condition | Stable capture window, colour/exposure checks, quality flags, repeatability controls |
| Camera movement or changing field of view | Registration, fixed ROI, reject non-comparable pairs |
| Occlusion mistaken for loss or growth of a habitat feature | Describe **visible coverage**, not physical ground area or biomass |
| Segmentation masks confuse flowers, grass, shrubs or litter | Feature-specific reference masks; taxonomy review; explicit unknowns |
| VLM invents plausible seasonal or weather-related changes | Image-only first pass; separate context; unchanged controls |
| Model output drifts with prompts or versions | Version prompts, weights, taxonomy, schema, and evaluation set |
| Overstated ecological validity | No biodiversity claims or ecological scoring validation until compared with independent measurements |

## Work plan and deliverables

**Phase 1: Define and assemble (initial)**  
Freeze taxonomy v0.1 and schemas; select three camera views; assemble quality-controlled images and metadata; create evaluation annotation guidance and a small reference set.

**Phase 2: Baselines**  
Run DeepSeek Flash and Qwen3.5-9B against the same image pairs. Record structured output validity, qualitative change labels, uncertainty, model/runtime configuration and costs.

**Phase 3: Quantification**  
Evaluate SAM 3 masks and visible coverage estimates in selected ROIs. Add image alignment/quality controls. Compute change deltas and thresholds deterministically.

**Phase 4: Validation and decision**  
Blindly score held-out results; inspect false positives and missed changes; test image-only versus context-aware outputs; document failures and recommended deployment strategy.

**Deliverables:** versioned taxonomy and JSON/TypeScript schema; annotated image-pair evaluation set; comparison results and error analysis; coverage/segmentation feasibility report; proposed scoring rules only if justified by accuracy; and a short recommendation on local versus hosted inference.

## Decision this experiment should support

Determine whether SPAIA can turn repeat photography into a sufficiently trustworthy **longitudinal record of visible habitat changes** to deploy alongside its insect-monitoring devices. If yes, use this evidence layer to support later studies of site management, seasonality, citizen observations and observed insect activity. If no, identify which classes need constrained segmentation, better optics, a narrower taxonomy or human verification.
